    The 5 tools

    Five ways to build more connection – for big feelings, clear limits and your own capacity.

    • Special Time

      Give your child 5–20 minutes in which they lead. This builds safety, trust and deep connection.

    • Setting Limits

      Stop off-track behaviour with love, closeness and respect — without punishment or shame.

    • Staylistening

      Stay present through big feelings, so your child can move through them and return to balance.

    • Playlistening

      Use play and laughter to release tension, fear or anger and bring more ease into family life.

    • Listening Partnership

      Exchange listening time with another parent to release emotions and create capacity for your child.

    About Julia

    I am Julia, a mother of three and a certified Hand in Hand Parenting Instructor. I know real family life — overwhelming mornings, meltdowns, sibling arguments and the moments when you wonder whose need should come first.

    I live what I teach. The tools have changed our family life — not because everything is easy now, but because we find our way back to one another sooner.

    Read more about my journey

    Being a mother was always a heartfelt wish. Yet at the beginning it was far harder than I expected. During my first pregnancy I worried whether I would be good enough and able to offer the closeness and safety I wanted for my child.

    While looking for guidance, I immersed myself in attachment-oriented parenting and personal development. When my son was born, everything became more intense. That was when I found Hand in Hand Parenting — and it fundamentally changed the way I parent.

    I have integrated these tools into our family life for years. There are still difficult moments and times when I fall back into old patterns. Listening Partnerships, coaching and meditation continue to be part of my own lifelong journey.

    In my courses I bring together the clear, loving tools of Hand in Hand with the inner capacity that grows as we understand ourselves better. Parents do not have to do this alone. This work carries us when we do it together.

    Frequently asked

    Good to know.

    Is the course right for me and my child?

    The course is for parents, grandparents and other important caregivers – especially of children aged around 2 to 10. If one of your children is older, the course can still be a good fit for your family: it is never too late to strengthen your connection. No prior knowledge is needed.

    If you are caring only for babies, older teenagers or adult children, please contact me before registering. Together, we can explore which support would suit your family.

    How does the course work and how much time do I need?

    We meet once a week for six weeks, for two to two and a half hours depending on the course you book. We discuss your experiences and questions, learn the tools and practise together. Between sessions, you try the tools in everyday life. Reading the additional materials is optional.

    You also arrange a weekly Listening Partnership with someone from the course: time to listen to each other. Allow 20–60 minutes in total, shared equally between you. Even ten minutes each is a good start. You pair up with someone who has a similar amount of time available and agree on the length together.

    What if I miss a session?

    If you miss a session, you receive a recording of the theory part and the written materials so you can catch up on the content. Only the theory is recorded; personal discussion, exercises and Listening are not recorded. You miss this shared practice when you cannot attend.

    Do I need childcare during the course?

    Yes, please arrange for time without your children and as few interruptions as possible. Exercises and Listening especially need time for yourself and a private space where you can speak openly.

    Breastfeeding a baby during the theory part is fine. Please arrange care for your baby as well during exercises and Listening.

    Can we attend together as parents?

    If you are raising a child together, you attend separate courses. This gives each of you your own space to speak openly – especially during Listening.

    Friends can attend the same course. Each person registers separately.
